Part 1 · Case study

From noisy results to a clean shortlist

Problem

Our AI-enabled CRM holds over 10k profiles, and we were running all of them through the LLM at once. Screening was slow, token costs were high, and the model hallucinated. Many of the "top" profiles were not the best fit.

My actions

  • Narrowed the focus to just 200 profiles instead of the full 10k. I filtered with LinkedIn Recruiter keywords and Boolean so every profile in the pool had the critical keywords.
  • Scraped 25 profiles at a time with Pulover's Macro.
  • Ran every batch through the same prompt: JD, hiring manager call notes, and 8-10 criteria.
  • Sent InMail the moment a batch produced a shortlist.

Result

  • About 2× faster end to end.
  • Token costs down to zero.
  • Fewer hallucinations, consistently top-quality shortlists.
  • Every 200 profiles gives about 20 strong candidates, with calls set immediately.
  • The whole process, from screening all 200 profiles to reaching out to the top 20, takes less than 30 minutes.

The workflow

Eight steps, repeated until all 200 are done

  1. Get the inputsThe JD, plus notes from a real call with the hiring manager.
  2. Write 8-10 criteriaMust have, good to have, must not have. Ranked by importance.
  3. Build the poolLinkedIn Recruiter, keywords and Boolean. About 200 profiles that contain every critical keyword.
  4. Scrape page 1Pulover's Macro opens 25 full profiles and saves them into one .txt file.
  5. Run the promptSame prompt, same criteria, on that .txt file.
  6. Pick the top profilesRead the ranking and take the strongest.
  7. InMail right awayReach out while the batch is fresh.
  8. Next pageScrape profiles 26-50, then repeat from step 4 until we finish all 200 profiles.
Steps 4 to 8 repeat 8 times: 200 profiles, 25 at a time. Start to finish, including InMail to the top 20, it takes less than 30 minutes.
Prompt engineering

Every prompt has four parts

JDThe full job description.
Call notesRaw notes from the hiring manager call: who they like, who to avoid, budget, red flags, what "technical" really means. This is where the real signal is.
Criteria list8-10 points tagged must have, good to have or must not have, ranked by importance.
The 25 full profilesThe scraped profiles from the current batch, pasted in full at the bottom of the prompt.

Criteria example: AI Delivery Lead 
Criteria:
The perfect candidate will have ALL of the below traits, ranking them in importance:
1. Residency Status (Non-Negotiable - Hard Gate)
Requirement: Must be a Singaporean Citizen or Permanent Resident (PR).
Exceptions: None.
2. Recent AI & GenAI Delivery Track Record (Non-Negotiable)
Recency: Proven delivery experience in GenAI, Agentic AI, or AI projects within the last 2 years.
3. Full-Lifecycle Consulting Expertise (Non-Negotiable)
Core Pillars (Must demonstrate all three):
Pitching: Presenting capabilities to senior external stakeholders and C-suite executives on AI programmes.
Solutioning: Leading discovery, ideation, and high-level AI/GenAI/Agentic architecture design with clients.
Delivery: Project managing end-to-end delivery as the primary bridge between client leadership and engineering teams.
4. Consulting Firm Background & Tier (Non-Negotiable)
Firm Quality: Must come from a reputable, non-tier 3 consulting firm (e.g., Accenture, Big 4).
MBB & Tier-1 Exception: Candidates from McKinsey, BCG, Bain, or Oliver Wyman are only viable if they have less than 5 years of total experience to fit budgetary constraints.
5. Experience Tenure & Hard Cap (Non-Negotiable)
Ideal Range: 6–10 years of total experience (e.g., profiles from Accenture).
Maximum Cap: Hard cap at 15 years of total experience.
Language Exception: Candidates with 15+ years of experience are excluded unless they are native Mandarin speakers.
6. Career Stability (Non-Negotiable)
Commitment: No "jumpy" career paths. Look for a clear history of tenure (ideally 3+ years per company) to ensure capability to see projects through in a startup environment.
7. Entrepreneurial Mindset & Startup Fit (Bonus / Differentiator)
Drive: Genuine enthusiasm for building a business rather than just delivering billable projects.
Innovation: Proactively stays ahead of AI developments and applies new techniques strategically to client problems.

What makes it work

Seven habits

Keep call notes messy and honest. Budget limits, firms to avoid and the hiring manager's gut feel change the ranking more than the JD does.

Be specific on seniority vs. firm tier. The same years of experience costs a different amount at Accenture than at MBB.

Spell out the proof you want. Real project implementation, sales exposure, agentic AI and LLM experience, not just buzzwords.

Fix your Boolean first. A tight 200-profile pool matters more than a clever prompt.

Keep batches at 25. Bigger batches bring hallucination back.

Reuse the prompt exactly. All 8 runs use the same prompt, so rankings stay comparable.

Reach out right after each batch. The best candidates are not waiting for you to finish.

Per 200 profiles: about 20 strong candidates, calls booked quickly, no extra token spend.
